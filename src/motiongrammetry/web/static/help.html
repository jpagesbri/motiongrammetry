How to

motiongrammetry turns a phone video walking around something into a clean, textured 3D model, a 3D-print file and more. This page explains every screen, step and option, with what to do when something goes wrong. In a hurry? Choose Assisted in the Run tab of the Generate screen: it walks you through all of this for your own capture.

The screenshots follow one example from start to print: a canvas shoe filmed on a stool, from the uCO3D dataset (Meta AI; Liu et al., 2025; CC BY 4.0), reconstructed and edited here.

How it works

Video→Keyframes→Masks→Camera positions→ Raw mesh→Clean mesh→Textured model→Exports, edits, prints

Each arrow is a stage. Every stage reads the previous one's result from disk, so you can re-run any stage with other options without starting over. A stage whose input changed after it ran is marked stale.

StageWhat it doesTypical time
framesKeeps the sharpest frames of the video, spread evenly along your path.< 1 min
maskAn AI model outlines the subject in every keyframe (subject vs background).1–3 min
sfmStructure from motion: works out where the camera was for every keyframe.1–3 min
mvsMulti-view stereo: a depth map per keyframe, fused into a dense cloud and a raw mesh (background included).5–30 min
vggtOptional AI alternative to sfm + mvs (needs a GPU). Faster, less detailed; replaces the camera positions.1–2 min
cleanCarves away what the masks call background, removes the floor or table, fills holes, keeps the main piece.~1 min
textureProjects the photos onto the clean mesh: the coloured model.1–5 min

Filming a good video

The video decides the quality more than any option. Two minutes spent filming well save an hour of fixing.

  • Walk slowly all the way around the subject, at one or two heights.
  • Keep the subject in the middle and fully in the frame, filling a good part of it.
  • Even, bright light; overcast daylight is ideal.
  • The subject stays perfectly still (people: lean against something, look at one point).
  • 20–60 seconds of video; landscape or portrait both work.
  • Something with texture around it (a newspaper under a plain object helps).
  • Fast movement or low light: motion blur ruins frames.
  • Zooming, or switching lenses mid-video.
  • Only filming one side: the other side cannot be reconstructed (you get a shell, not a full object).
  • Shiny, transparent, very dark or perfectly plain surfaces (few details to match).
  • The subject moving (people breathing is fine; turning the head is not).
  • Spinning on the spot instead of walking around.

Quick start

  1. Install and start it: ./install.sh in the project folder (Docker; it checks everything, asks about the optional VGGT step and opens http://127.0.0.1:8765). Later: ./install.sh stop, ./install.sh update. For development without Docker: uv run mg ui.
  2. New scan (left): give it a name, then Upload video… (or paste a path to a video on this computer and press Create).
  3. In the Run tab, either follow Assisted, or switch to Manual, tick the stages and press Run selected.
  4. Follow the progress there; when texture is done, open the Preview tab: the model, its keyframes and masks, and the files.
  5. Check that the model stands the right way (the assisted run asks; otherwise Orient… in the Preview), then Export….

Assisted run

A guided run for your own capture: choose Assisted at the top of the Run tab (Manual is the same screen, see below). It asks what you filmed and how, picks starting options (and tells you why each one), then goes one stage at a time: what the stage does, every option with its explanation, the suggested value and the reason. After each stage it reads the results and says whether something should change, with an Apply & re-run button. The last step, Place your model, checks that the model stands the right way before you export it.

While a step runs, its page shows the sub-steps (done, running with the time left, still to come), a small picture of the latest result, the log on request (Show log) and Stop this step (click twice). Switching to Manual, to the Preview tab or to another scan never stops a run: only Stop does. If a step finishes while you are in the Preview, a notice tells you, with its suggestions. The assistant remembers where you were for each scan, also after a reload.

Examples of what it catches: few keyframes or a blurred video; masks that missed the subject in some frames; cameras that did not connect (and the mapper / matcher that fixes it); a clean step that carved away parts of the subject where the masks were unsure (it measures how sure they were); quarter-resolution depth maps; a scan filmed from one side only.

For frames, masks and cameras, Show the flagged keyframes opens the frame viewer on the keyframes that look wrong.

Scans

A scan is one video and everything made from it (on disk: a folder under workspaces/). Each shows a thumbnail (a snapshot of the 3D model once there is one, a keyframe before), its size and when it last ran; hover for a larger card. The dots are the stages that are done. Progress and the log

A running step lists its sub-steps: done (with their time), running (with progress and the time left) and still to come. how the last run went on a finished step shows the same for its last run. The Log drawer at the bottom has the live output and a History of every run; it is closed unless you open it (a dot means new output) and opens by itself when a step fails.

Preview: 3D model

Layers: camera positions, sparse points (from sfm), dense cloud and raw mesh (from mvs), clean mesh, textured model and, if you edited it, the custom version. Drag to orbit, right-drag or middle-drag to pan, wheel to zoom. Click a keyframe to look through its camera, double-click it to open it big. The view cube (bottom left, in every 3D view) shows which way you are looking, Z up as in the print files: click a face to look straight at it, near an edge or a corner for the 45° view in between; the button next to it frames the model, and Persp / Ortho (or 5) switches between perspective and orthographic (no foreshortening: sizes and parallel lines stay true, handy for judging proportions and orientation). Display (W cycles): Colours, Solid grey (judge the shape without the photo), Wireframe, or Solid + wires (the shape and how dense the mesh is; the wires fade in as you zoom, since on a dense scan they would cover everything). Highlight holes draws every opening in orange and counts them: that is what the print report means by lids. Export says what the scan has ready (and whether some of it is stale), shows the print check, and opens Export….

Orientation

The model is stood up automatically (on the table or floor when one is found, else from how the camera moved). When that is wrong, Orient… (or the assisted run's last step) fixes it: Click the floor… then click the spot that should rest on the floor (a curved base, like a sole, settles onto the points it would really rest on, so it stands without a stand); Turn 90° about any axis; orbit until you face the model's front and press This view is the front; or free Rotate / Move handles. Centre and Drop to floor tidy up; turns do it for you. Save and every export uses it; files exported before are marked outdated in the Export window and made again when you ask. Reset to automatic forgets it. Nothing is re-run and the meshes are not changed. If you re-run sfm, the cameras change and your orientation no longer applies: set it again.

Preview: frames and masks

Every keyframe big: Photo, Overlay (the background veiled), Compare (drag the line: photo on one side, mask on the other), Side by side or Mask. ← → step through them, the wheel zooms, drag pans, double-click fits. Each keyframe says how sharp it is against its neighbours, how much of it the mask covers and whether sfm placed its camera. Flagged steps only through the ones worth a look: blurrier than their neighbours, no camera, a mask that found nothing or that jumps in size against its neighbours (it caught background or lost part of the subject). View from this camera shows the 3D model from that keyframe's position.

Export

Files are made when you ask for them, not by the pipeline: each is kept, so asking again takes no time (the row says ready), until something it is made from changes (the mesh, the texture, the orientation) or you change its settings, and then it says outdated and is made again. Tick what you want to take out of a scan and pick each one's format; several come as one zip, a folder each (every file is also downloadable on its own).

Export…

Everything exported together is placed the same way, so the pieces line up. A height sets the clean model's real size (GLB in metres, the others in millimetres; a scan has no scale of its own), and everything else is scaled with it. Up is your saved orientation when you set one (see Orientation), else automatic; the other choices are for one-off files. The cameras stay in the reconstruction's frame, as COLMAP tools expect. The 3D view shows each file (pick one at the top left when there are several); a dense cloud of hundreds of MB is only downloaded. Your choices are remembered.

3D printing

A scan is a surface, often with holes and pieces; a printer needs one closed solid that stands. The print file is rebuilt on a ~0.3 mm grid, which removes self-intersections and thickens walls thinner than the minimum wall.

SettingMeaning
Print as solidOpenings are closed with smooth lids: a full object. Good when you filmed all around.
Print as shellOnly the scanned surface, given a thickness: for scans of one side (a face filmed from the front). Nothing is invented.
Print as autoShell when lids would invent more than a third of the surface, otherwise solid.
Shell grows inwardThe scanned surface stays exactly where it was measured; material is added behind it. Outward or both ways soften the detail.
StandA thin disc is added under the model when it would topple over.
Flat baseThe bottom share of the height is flattened so a solid stands without wobbling.

Whatever could still go wrong (invented share, stand, thin walls, separate pieces, overhangs needing supports) is listed with the exported print and, in the Preview's Export panel, as the print check of the last print exported (marked outdated once the mesh or the orientation changes), each with the option that changes it. A scan's print settings (print_height, print_mode, …, set by the assisted run from what you filmed) are where the Export window starts.

Edit & paint

Edits are saved as a custom version (in mesh/custom/); the pipeline's files are never changed and Delete custom version goes back to them. The two modes work on different things: Sculpt mesh changes the shape, Paint texture changes the colours on that shape. So the order is always sculpt → save → texture the edit → paint.

After you save a sculpt, the textured model you can paint is still the one made before your edit: it has the old shape, so pieces you removed still show and holes you closed are missing. Paint mode says so and offers two ways to give your edited shape a texture of its own:

Texture my editsCarry the texture over
HowThe photos are projected again, onto your edited meshYour old texture is copied onto the edited mesh, nearest point to nearest point
TakesAbout a minute on the GPUSeconds to half a minute
Colour of new surfaceWhat the cameras saw; where no camera saw it (a closed underside, a filled gap) it is greyTaken from the surface next to it, so closed holes and smoothed areas blend in
Best forRemoving or reshaping things, and you want the real photo detail on what is leftClosing holes, smoothing, filling; you want the model to look unchanged, and quickly
NeedsThe photos and OpenMVS's scene (mg mvs); not available on VGGT scansOnly the old texture; works on every scan

Either way the result becomes your custom textured model (next to your custom mesh), the pipeline's own textured model is left alone, and paint saved before that is replaced (you are asked first). Then paint. If it comes out wrong, Undo layout goes back from a carry-over; re-running texture goes back to the photos.

Mesh check and Sanitize mesh

Some meshes break texturing or spoil it: two faces on the same three points, wound opposite ways (a pillow; OpenMVS crashes on them), bow-tie vertices where two fans of faces touch at a point, edges with three or more faces, faces with no area, pieces that flatten to no area (Carry the texture over can't lay them out) and faces wound against their neighbours. Paint checks the textured model and your edited mesh when it opens, and Texture my edits and Carry the texture over check again before they start: each problem is named with how much of the mesh it affects and the fix. Sanitize mesh… there takes you to Sculpt with the fixes for them ticked; you can also go ahead anyway.

In Sculpt, Sanitize mesh… (under Mesh) checks the mesh as it is now, unsaved edits included, and lists the fixes with what it found for each (in amber when there is something to fix) and how long it will take:

FixWhat it does
Remove degenerate and duplicate facesFaces with no area, the same face twice, pillows (of a pillow the face wound like its neighbours stays)
Make it manifoldRemoves bow-tie vertices and the extra faces on edges shared by three or more. That opens small holes: tick Close holes too
Remove small loose piecesPieces smaller than the share of the largest piece's area you give
Recompute normals and orientationEvery face wound the same way as its neighbours, each piece facing out
Close holesThe holes whose rim is at most the given share of the model's size (its bounding box diagonal) long, with the same lids as Close all holes
Remove unreferenced verticesPoints no face uses

The fixes run in that order, as one undo step, and the window then says what each changed and checks the result again. Save keeps it. The times are measured on this computer's processor (these fixes don't use the GPU): a short test the first time you open the window, then the fixes you run, scaled to the mesh's number of faces. Closing holes also depends on how big the holes are.

Below the fixes the window also says how many faces cut through other faces (self-intersections), and in how many spots. Sanitize doesn't change them, and they don't break texturing; where the surface folds over itself they can show as a crease. Show them closes the window and marks them in red on the model, through the surface; ◀ ▶ take the view from spot to spot, the largest first, and Hide (or Esc) takes the marks away, as does any edit. Smooth or carve the spots that matter, or Merge with whole mesh, which rebuilds the whole surface without them.

Sculpt

Left-drag on the mesh to use the tool, off it (or right-drag) to orbit, middle-drag to pan, wheel to zoom. The example: the shoe sat on a stool, so its underside was never filmed and the scan is open there, with a loose scrap hanging under the toe. Two tools and a save give it a sole.

Display (W) and highlight holes work as in the Preview: Solid + wires after Merge shows how fine the new mesh is; holes update as you cut and fill.

ToolKeyWhat it does
Draw / Dig inD CRaise / push in the surface
Inflate / DeflateI OSwell / shrink along the surface
SmoothSRelax bumps and noise (hold Shift with any tool)
Flatten, Pinch, GrabF P GPress flat, sharpen creases, drag a region
DecimateESimplify where you brush, down to the Density (÷2 is half the scan's vertices): when you let go, short edges are merged, shortest first, never folding a triangle; open edges of the mesh and everything outside the stroke stay as they are
Cut awayXDelete surface (tick cap the opening to close the hole it leaves)
Fill + MergeBBrush spheres into a hole or gap, then Merge fill: only the area around them is rebuilt (at the mesh's own resolution) and stitched in; the rest keeps every detail. An opening the spheres only partly cover is closed whole. Tick Merge: whole mesh to rebuild everything at Detail instead (merges separate shells, removes inner walls)
RestoreRBrush over the raw mesh (amber ghost) to bring back what clean carved away
Plane cutVDrag a line: cut straight through (Enter to apply). A new opening is closed flat; one that joins an opening the scan already had is left for Close all holes

Adaptive detail (a tick box under the brush): when the mesh is too coarse to shape, each dab first adds vertices around itself, up to the Density below it, and splits the triangles beside them too, so no crack opens. The density is a factor of the mesh where you brush, as it is when you start the stroke: ×2 (the default) is twice the vertices it had there, ×4 four times with edges half as long, up to ×8. A coarse part gets the same factor of its own density, and what earlier strokes did counts: after Decimate ÷4, ×1.5 adds about half to what Decimate left. One stroke counts once: going over the same area again before you let go adds nothing more, while a new stroke adds its factor again (×1.5 twice is ×2.25). It doesn't depend on the zoom or the brush size. It works with Draw, Dig in, Inflate, Deflate, Smooth, Flatten and Pinch, on the surface under the brush only (not the other side of a thin part). One stroke adds at most 50,000 vertices and says so when it stops; brush again to add more. A stroke and the vertices it added undo together. Decimate uses the same control the other way (÷), also from the mesh as it is: ÷2 after Adaptive detail halves the refined area, and ÷2 twice is ÷4.

Absolute (the switch under the slider; the default is Relative) sets a target instead of a change: the row reads Target, and both brushes aim at that share of the scan's typical density (from 13% to 800%: 200% is twice as dense as the scan, 50% half), whatever the mesh is now. Adaptive detail adds only where the mesh is sparser than the target and Decimate merges only where it is denser, so you can cover an area in as many strokes as you need, and parts made denser or sparser before meet at the same density. Use it to even out an area; relative to the mesh (the default) to make a part finer or coarser than it is (Relative). Each mode keeps its own value.

Also: Close all holes (each lid is an even mesh that follows its rim: flat where the rim is flat, curved where it rises), Remove loose pieces, [ ] brush size, Ctrl inverts, Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z undo and redo, Ctrl+S save.

Paint

Brushes: paint, restore the original photo, blur, smudge, clone (Ctrl+click sets the source), lighten, darken, saturate, desaturate and a colour picker (Alt+click anywhere). Painting on the model only reaches surface you can see and crosses texture seams. Re-running texture replaces the paint.

Clone on the model copies by place on the surface, not by position in the texture (which is cut into many small pieces): Ctrl+click the top right corner of one side, start painting at the top right corner of another, and that side gets the first one's look, corner to corner, even when the two face different ways. The amber ring shows where it is copying from; the source must be in view. Later strokes keep the same alignment, so a side can be filled in several strokes, until you Ctrl+click a new source. On the flat texture, clone copies by position in the texture.

Texture layout: OpenMVS cuts the texture by the photo each bit came from, so a scan's texture is thousands of tiny pieces (20,000 for a head) and the flat texture is hard to paint on. Rebuild layout cuts it again by the model's sides: faces stay together while they face the same way (measured on a smoothed copy, so the scan's noise does not count), Angle is how far one piece may turn and Min. piece the smallest piece, as a share of the surface (smaller ones join their neighbour). Each piece is flattened and packed into a new image, and the colours are copied across triangle by triangle at the same level of detail: the model looks the same, the flat texture shows whole parts (about 250 pieces for that head, a minute to rebuild). Count pieces tells you the number first, and Preview shows the new texture at low resolution in the flat view, in a few seconds, without saving anything. It is saved as your custom texture; save or discard the paint before. Undo layout goes back to the layout it replaced, until you save paint or re-run texture. Re-running texture brings back OpenMVS's layout.

Edit seams… draws the texture's seams on the model in amber, to change them by hand (best after a Rebuild layout: OpenMVS's own layout is a wall of lines). Cut: drag along the model and the seam follows the mesh's edges between the points you pass; a closed loop makes a new piece, and an open cut lets a curved piece (a cone, a cap) lie flat. Join: brush over seams to remove them, on the surface facing you; the pieces either side become one. Ctrl+Z undoes a stroke. With colour the pieces each piece your seams make shows in its own colour, with the count, after every stroke. Preview texture shows the texture they would make in the flat view, nothing saved. Apply seams rebuilds the layout with exactly the pieces your seams enclose, flattened, packed and recoloured as above; a piece that cannot lie flat is cut further, and the message says how many were. Undo layout goes back.

Troubleshooting

What you seeLikely causeWhat to do
Parts of a person or object are missing after cleanThe masks were unsure there and carving was strictRaise bg_tolerance (0.35) and re-run clean; or Restore in the editor. The assisted run detects this.

disk-low before mvsDepth maps need several GBRaise resolution_level or the low-disk preset

Every warning on a stage card comes with a hint. The Log → History tab keeps the full log of every run.

Glossary

KeyframeA frame kept from the video for reconstruction.
MaskThe subject's outline in a keyframe (white subject, black background).
SfMStructure from motion: camera positions and a sparse point cloud from matched features.
MVSMulti-view stereo: dense depth from the positioned cameras, then a mesh.
CarvingRemoving mesh parts that the masks see as background in enough views.
StaleA result older than its inputs (an earlier stage ran again since).
Custom versionYour edited mesh or texture, kept beside the pipeline's.
ShellA print of the scanned surface only, given a thickness.
